## 4. The permit sequence gets easier, and you should exploit that

**SBC Permit No. SBC-2026-RDP-10-AIO** was issued 28 July 2026 and is **valid only until
27 July 2027**. Clause 7.2 records that it does **not** currently cover the Project's title,
scope, personnel or institutions, and Clause 7.1 makes UTS responsible for maintaining
validity, including renewals through SORAS.

Under the original 1 Dec 2026 start, you would have been seeking a variation during Phase 1 and
then a renewal a few months later — two SORAS submissions in overlapping windows, with
sampling blocked until the first landed. That was the tightest regulatory sequence in the
project and the most likely cause of a six-month slip.

**The delay turns this from a problem into an advantage.** Clause 5.2 expressly permits
preparatory steps between the Effective Date and the Commencement Date, naming the SORAS
application for the permit variation as one of them. So:

- **Submit the variation *and* the renewal through SORAS now**, during the gap, combined if SBC
  permits it. You have roughly four months of preparatory window before 1 March 2027.
- If the permit is varied and renewed **before** the project starts, sampling can begin in
  month one instead of month six.
- Fold the final title into that application, so the permit, the MoA and the proposal all carry
  the same project description from the outset. This is exactly why the title decision should be
  made now rather than after submission.

One caution: a renewal granted in, say, December 2026 may run only to a fixed date rather than
24 months, so check what period SBC grants on renewal and plan for a further renewal inside the
project. Put that in the milestone table as a named task with an owner.

## 5. The science genuinely improves — say so in the proposal

This is the part worth being pleased about. My earlier review raised **single-cycle design** as
a real weakness: one Northeast monsoon and one Southwest monsoon means n≈1 per season, with no
way to separate your effect from an anomalous year.

A 1 March 2027 start with a 24-month term runs to **end February 2029**, which spans:

| Season | Approximate window | In project? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Southwest monsoon 2027 | May – Sep 2027 | yes |
| Northeast monsoon 2027/28 | Nov 2027 – Mar 2028 | yes |
| Southwest monsoon 2028 | May – Sep 2028 | yes |
| Northeast monsoon 2028/29 | Nov 2028 – Feb 2029 | yes |

**Two full cycles of each monsoon instead of one.** That gives you inter-annual replication,
makes "seasonal variation" a conclusion rather than an assumption, and lets you fit the
repeated-measures models the draft already promises. It also means a haze season that fails to
materialise in one year does not cost you the contrast.

Write this into the proposal as a design feature. *"The 24-month window spans two complete
monsoon cycles, permitting assessment of inter-annual variability in community composition
rather than a single-cycle snapshot"* is a strength a reviewer will credit — and it is only
true because of the slip.
